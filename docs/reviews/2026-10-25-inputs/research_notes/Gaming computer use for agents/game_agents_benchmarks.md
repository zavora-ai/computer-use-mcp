# Game-playing agents through screens and keyboard/mouse/controller: systems, benchmarks, interfaces (state as of 2026-10-25)

Scope note: these notes cover 2022-2026 work. Unless a finding is marked "(secondary)", it comes from the primary paper, project page or vendor post. Secondary sources are flagged where the primary was unavailable (paywall, 403) or not found.

## 1. Which game-playing agents exist, and how do they observe and act?

### Takeaway
Two families exist. The first is **frontier-LLM harnesses**: Cradle, Claude Plays Pokemon, Gemini Plays Pokemon, VideoGameBench's VG-Agent and the PokeAgent baselines. They see a screenshot or a short clip every few seconds, act through coarse primitives (button presses, key holds, click-at-coordinate) and usually **pause the game or play turn-based games**. The second is **trained vision-language-action policies**: SIMA 1/2, Lumine, Game-TARS, UI-TARS-2, NitroGen and VPT. They run a small, fast model at 5-30 Hz and emit **relative mouse deltas, key-down/up state per short chunk, or gamepad stick/button vectors**. Only this second family plays real-time 3D games for hours, and most of its members still freeze the game clock, or train for latency, to manage it.

### Cited Findings

**Cradle (BAAI / Skywork et al., "General Computer Control"; v1 5 Mar 2024, v3 2 Jul 2024)**
- Input is screenshots; output is "executable code for low-level keyboard and mouse control". The environments are four commercial games (Red Dead Redemption 2, Cities: Skylines, Stardew Valley, Dealer's Life 2), five apps (Chrome, Outlook, Feishu, Meitu, CapCut) and OSWorld — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03186)
- The six modules are Information Gathering, Self-Reflection, Task Inference, Skill Curation, Action Planning and Memory (episodic plus procedural). The backbone is GPT-4o (gpt-4o-2024-05-13) at temperature 0, with text-embedding-ada-002 for skill retrieval — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3)
- Observation is **a video clip of the last action's execution recorded at 2 fps (a screenshot every 0.5 s)**, not a single screenshot — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3)
- The action primitives are key_press, key_hold, key_release, mouse_move (with a **speed** parameter, which the authors say matters in RDR2), wheel_scroll, and mouse click/hold/release — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3)
- **RDR2 and Stardew Valley are paused while waiting for backbone model responses**: "we must pause those game environments while waiting for backbone model responses". Other environments run continuously — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3)
- The abstract claims Cradle is the first agent to "complete 40-minute-long real missions" in RDR2 and to "create a city of a thousand people" in Cities: Skylines, with an 87% maximal weekly profit in Dealer's Life 2 — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03186). The paper body reports a lower Cities: Skylines maximal population of 450±224 (850±142 with human assistance), so the "thousand people" claim appears to rest on the best run or on assisted runs. Dealer's Life 2 is 39.6±27.3% mean total profit with an 87.4% single-run peak — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3)
- RDR2 per-task success, out of 5 runs: Follow Dutch 5/5, Follow Micah 5/5, Hitch Horse 4/5, Protect Dutch 1/5, Search for Supplies 1/5. Stardew Cultivation is 4/5 and Shopping 1/5 — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3)

**Google DeepMind SIMA 1 (13 Mar 2024) and SIMA 2 (blog 13 Nov 2025; tech report 4 Dec 2025)**
- SIMA 1: pixels in, keyboard-and-mouse out, "the same screen observations that a human playing the game would", with no game-state access. The policy outputs "keyboard-and-mouse actions for sequences of 8 actions" (chunking). Environments are **asynchronous and real-time (not paused)**; the authors train with action offsets to absorb latency and use buffering, caching and TPU scheduling to reduce it — [SIMA 1 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10179v2)
- SIMA 1 games: Goat Simulator 3, Hydroneer, No Man's Sky, Satisfactory, Teardown, Valheim, Wobbly Life, plus research environments (Construction Lab, Playhouse, ProcTHOR, WorldLab). On a No Man's Sky subset, human experts scored 60%, SIMA 34% and a no-language baseline 11% — [SIMA 1 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10179v2); [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/sima-generalist-ai-agent-for-3d-virtual-environments/)
- SIMA 2 is built on **Gemini Flash-Lite**, chosen "due to the latency constraints that come with embodied action". Input is a stream of **720p RGB frames**. Output is structured text that parses deterministically into **96 keyboard keys, mouse clicks, and discretized relative (x, y) mouse movements**, emitted in action chunks — [SIMA 2 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04797v1)
- SIMA 2 "effectively doubles the average success rate of SIMA 1, approaching human-level performance" in training environments. On held-out MineDojo it completed tasks in 26 of 50 categories, against two task types for SIMA 1. Untrained Gemini baselines through the same interface reached 3.2% (Flash-Lite) and 7.0% (Pro) across 10 training domains — [SIMA 2 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04797v1)
- Self-improvement: a Gemini task-setter proposes tasks and a Gemini reward model scores trajectories from 0 to 100 (50 or above counts as success). In ASKA the agent went from under a quarter of tasks to roughly the human reference average — [SIMA 2 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04797v1)

**ByteDance Seed Lumine (Genshin Impact; arXiv 12 Nov 2025)**
- Built on Qwen2-VL-7B-Base. **Observation: one 1280×720 frame every 200 ms (5 Hz). Actions: 30 Hz, as six 33 ms action chunks per 200 ms step** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892)
- **Mouse is relative (ΔX, ΔY) in (-1000, 1000) and is executed over the 200 ms step; scroll ΔZ is in [-5, 5]. Each chunk lists 0-4 keys; unlisted keys are released, and keys present in consecutive chunks stay held without re-pressing** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892)
- "Hybrid thinking": the model decides per step whether to emit a reasoning trace, so it "adaptively invokes reasoning only when necessary". Reasoning text also acts as long-term memory, and a 20-frame sliding window is the short-term memory (10 frames was best in ablation) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08892); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892)
- It completes the five-hour Mondstadt main storyline "on par with human-level efficiency". Zero-shot it handles a 100-minute Wuthering Waves mission and the five-hour first chapter of Honkai: Star Rail — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08892)
- Training data: 2,424 h raw gameplay, of which 1,731 h was used for pre-training, plus 200 h of instruction data and 15 h (15K traces) of reasoning data. Pre-training took 12.4 days on 64 H100s — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892)

**ByteDance Seed Game-TARS (arXiv Oct 2025)**
- The unified action space is three primitives tied to physical input, **`mouseMove(dx, dy)`, `mouseClick(button)` and `keyPress(keys)`**, plus Think, LongThinking and No-Op. Actions are "time-aligned" to recorded input at **50-100 ms intervals**: operations between frames are merged into one action event and gaps become no-ops. Mouse deltas are capped at |180| — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1)
- Scale: 526B tokens of continual pre-training (~20k game hours, ~100 games), and 40B post-training tokens (~2k hours, ~500 games). Variants are MoE-mini (2.5B active), MoE-Large and a dense Qwen2.5-VL-7B — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1)
- The authors note that inference latency **required packaging web games into a sandbox with time-pause** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1)

**ByteDance Seed UI-TARS-2 (arXiv 2 Sep 2025)**
- Games are played "through a human-like action space (mouse clicks, key presses, and scrolling)" in "a browser-sandboxed, screenshot-only setting" (HTML5/WebGL mini-games) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02544v2)

**NVIDIA NitroGen (arXiv 2601.02427, Jan 2026; NVIDIA with Stanford, Caltech, UChicago, UT Austin)**
- A 500M-parameter flow-matching policy adapted from GR00T N1, trained by behaviour cloning on **40,000 h of gameplay video across 1,000+ games**. Actions were **extracted from on-screen gamepad input overlays** in streamer videos (joystick R² 0.84, button frame accuracy 0.96) — [project page](https://nitrogen.minedojo.org/); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427)
- Input is a single 256×256 RGB frame (SigLIP 2, 256 tokens); more past frames gave no benefit. **Output is a gamepad action: a 16-dim binary button vector (d-pad, face, shoulders, triggers, thumb, start, back) plus 4 continuous joystick values, in chunks of 16 actions** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427). Sources disagree on the tensor shape: the appendix says 16×24, and a Hugging Face model-card mirror says "21x16 … 17 binary buttons" — [HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/aoiandroid/NitroGen)
- The "universal simulator" **"intercepts the game engine's system clock to control simulation time"**, so the Gymnasium API freezes the game while the model predicts. Real-time and asynchronous deployment is left to future work — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427)
- It works best on gamepad-native genres (action, platformer, racing) and is weaker on mouse/keyboard-heavy RTS and MOBA; it supports Windows games — [HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/aoiandroid/NitroGen); [Tom's Hardware](https://tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/nvidia-led-nitrogen-is-a-generalist-video-gaming-ai-that-can-play-any-title-research-also-has-big-implications-for-robotics)

**Microsoft Muse / WHAM (Nature, Feb 2025)**
- Muse is a "World and Human Action Model" from Microsoft Research and Ninja Theory. It generates game visuals, controller actions or both, and was trained on Bleeding Edge gameplay: over 1 billion images and controller actions, about 7 years of continuous play. It is a world model for gameplay ideation, not a deployed desktop agent — [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/introducing-muse); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/microsoft-reveals-new-gaming-focused-generative-ai-model-muse-that-could-revive-classic-games/)

**Minecraft agents**
- **VPT (OpenAI, 23 Jun 2022)**: uses the "native human interface" of mouse and keyboard **at 20 Hz**. An inverse dynamics model trained on about 2,000 h of contractor data labels about 70,000 h of internet video. It is the first agent to craft diamond tools, a task that takes proficient humans about 24,000 actions (20+ minutes) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11795); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/vpt)
- **Voyager (May 2023)**: a GPT-4 agent acting through **executable code** rather than pixels, with an automatic curriculum, a skill library of code, and iterative prompting with execution errors and self-verification. It reports 3.3× more unique items, 2.3× longer distances and up to 15.3× faster tech-tree milestones than the prior SOTA — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291). (Orak's Minecraft environment likewise uses "executable JavaScript within the Mineflayer environment" — [Orak HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03610v3))
- **JARVIS-1 (Nov 2023)**: multimodal LM planner, goal-conditioned controllers and multimodal memory; "nearly perfect" on 200+ entry-to-intermediate MCU tasks; 12.5% on the long-horizon diamond pickaxe — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05997v3)
- **Optimus-3 (Jun 2025)**: a Mixture-of-Experts Minecraft agent; v2 reports self-claimed gains of 21% on Planning, 3.4× on Grounding and 60% success on open-ended tasks — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10357v1); [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10357v2)
- **MineStudio (Dec 2024)**: an open toolkit with seven components (simulator, data, model, offline pre-training, online fine-tuning, inference, benchmark) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18293)

**Frontier-LLM Pokémon harnesses**
- **Claude Plays Pokémon** launched on Twitch in Feb 2025 with Claude 3.7 Sonnet, which won three gym badges; Claude 3.5 Sonnet "failed the very beginning" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/25/anthropics-claude-ai-is-playing-pokemon-on-twitch-slowly/)
- Claude's tools (secondary): press buttons in sequence, navigate to coordinates on screen, and update a knowledge base — [BGR](https://bgr.com/entertainment/claude-ai-playing-pokemon-red-might-be-your-next-twitch-obsession/); [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/?p=64294)
- The open starter harness uses the PyBoy emulator: each step it "captures a screenshot", "reads the game state information from memory", and sends both to Claude, which returns emulator commands — [GitHub starter](https://github.com/davidhershey/ClaudePlaysPokemonStarter)
- As of April 2025, both the Claude and Gemini harnesses gave overlaid screenshots, key RAM information, a way to save text, a text-to-button-press tool, a pathfinding tool, occasional context summarization, and a critic or guide model instance. Claude's navigator moves the player to "a valid set of coordinates from the screenshot overlay grid", so it can only target on-screen tiles — [LessWrong, Bradshaw, 19 Apr 2025](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7mqp8uRnnPdbBzJZE/is-gemini-now-better-than-claude-at-pokemon)
- The Opus 4.5 harness changes included Surf support in the navigator and marking spin and teleport tiles non-navigable. Notably, **the harness waits for the player to stop moving at a spin tile before sending the next screenshot** (frame synchronisation) — [harness-doc excerpt on LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/users/joshua-levine)
- Anthropic describes the Pokémon agent as an example of structured note-taking: "maintains precise tallies across thousands of game steps", builds maps of explored regions, and "After context resets, the agent reads its own notes" — [Anthropic Engineering, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- **Gemini Plays Pokémon** (independent developer Joel Zhang) gives a **textual minimap** and more detailed text labels. A separate Gemini instance does pathfinding by "mentally simulat[ing] a BFS" (added 14 Apr 2025 for the Rocket Hideout maze) — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7mqp8uRnnPdbBzJZE/is-gemini-now-better-than-claude-at-pokemon)
- The Gemini 2.5 technical report (as summarised by Drew Breunig) describes a `pathfinder` tool backed by an XML map model populated during exploration, a `boulder_puzzle_strategist` for a single puzzle, and a goals scratchpad. Gemini 2.5 Pro "struggled to utilize the raw pixels of the Game Boy screen directly", so **the harness extracted text from RAM**, and the model played "roughly as well as without the vision information" — [dbreunig.com](https://www.dbreunig.com/2025/06/17/an-agentic-case-study-playing-pok%C3%A9mon-with-gemini.html); [Gemini 2.5 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06261)
- "Continual Harness" (Karten, Zhang et al., 11 May 2026) automates harness refinement: starting from a minimal interface, the agent alternates between acting and revising its own prompt, sub-agents, skills and memory within a single run. It recovers most of the gap to a hand-engineered harness on Pokémon Red and Emerald — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09998)

**Real-time reasoning architectures**
- AgileThinker (Real-Time Reasoning Gym; arXiv Nov 2025, ICLR 2026) "runs two LLMs in two parallel threads". A planning thread streams long reasoning while a reactive thread must output an action within the environment's update interval and can read the planner's partial reasoning — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04898v1)
- "Reflex First, Reflect Later" (arXiv Jun 2025) converts inference delay into equivalent simulation frames, and pairs fast reflexive policies with an asynchronous LLM reflector — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07223) (abstract only)

### Inferences
- The systems that actually finish long real-time 3D games (Lumine, SIMA 2, NitroGen, Game-TARS) are all **purpose-trained small models** (0.5B-7B, or Flash-Lite) with sub-second loops. Frontier general models reach those games only through heavy harnesses and pausing (Cradle, VideoGameBench Lite).
- A general MCP server is mostly serving the first family: frontier models calling tools at multi-second intervals. Its job is to make each call carry more time-precise motor content (chunks with holds and deltas) and to return better-synchronised observations, rather than to reach 30 Hz itself.

### Gaps
- I found no first-party OpenAI publication on CUA/Operator playing games. The only quantified CUA game numbers are third-party (UI-TARS-2's 15-game table, below).
- The SIMA 2 control rate (Hz), chunk length and latency are not in the text portions I could read. SIMA 1 states 8-action chunks but no Hz.
- Muse's 10 fps and 300×180 figures appear only in press coverage; I could not reach the Nature paper (login redirect) to confirm the action discretisation or rate.
- Exact Claude Plays Pokémon tool schemas (button-press tool arguments, wait frames) are not in the starter README, and the full harness changelog document was not accessible.

## 2. Benchmarks and headline scores of frontier models

### Takeaway
On raw-pixel, real-time play, frontier models score near zero: VideoGameBench 0.48% full and 1.6% Lite (2025), ARC-AGI-3 under 1% at launch (Mar 2026), and roughly 0% on PokeAgent speedrunning without a harness. Scores jump when the harness supplies text state, memory, pathfinding and pausing (lmgame-Bench: 40% → 86.7% of runs beating random; ARC-AGI-3: 7.8% → 38.3% for the same model under a different harness, per a secondary source). Trained computer-use models beat general CUAs on browser games (UI-TARS-2 59.8 vs OpenAI CUA 24.7 vs Claude Computer Use 21.6, normalised to human = 100).

### Cited Findings

**VideoGameBench (Zhang, Griffiths, Narasimhan, Press; 23 May 2025, v3 14 May 2026)**
- 10 test games (7 public, 3 secret) from Game Boy/GBC and MS-DOS, plus a 13-game dev set. The public test games are Kirby's Dream Land DX, Pokémon Crystal, Link's Awakening DX, Civilization I, The Need for Speed, The Incredible Machine and Doom II. Lite-eligible games are Doom II, Kirby and Link's Awakening — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- Overall completion is **GPT-4o 0.09% full / 1.6% Lite; Claude 3.7 Sonnet 0.48% / 1.6%; Gemini 2.5 Pro 0.48% / 1.6%**; Llama 4, Gemini 2.0 Flash and Qwen2.5-VL 7B/32B score 0% / 0% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- Practice games (10 targets each), as location clicking / mouse dragging / 2D navigation: Claude 3.7 Sonnet 100% / 10% / 80%; Gemini 2.5 Pro 100% / 0% / 50%; GPT-4o 30% / 0% / 30%. **Every model is at or near zero on mouse dragging** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- Scoring uses equal weight per game, split across checkpoints that are detected by perceptual-hash matching against walkthrough frames (Hamming distance under 12) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- I found no newer public leaderboard with GPT-5, Claude 4.x or Gemini 3 scores on VideoGameBench.

**BALROG (Paglieri et al.; Nov 2024, ICLR 2025)**
- Environments: BabyAI, Crafter, TextWorld, Baba Is AI, MiniHack and NetHack. Actions are natural-language strings with 16 observations of history — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13543v2)
- Language-only average progress: Claude 3.5 Sonnet 32.64%, GPT-4o 32.34%, Llama 3.1 70B 27.88%. Vision-language: Claude 3.5 Sonnet 35.48%, Gemini 1.5 Pro 25.76%, GPT-4o 22.56%. GPT-4o loses about 10 points when given images — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13543v2)
- On NetHack the best model, o1-preview, reached about 1.57% progression — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13543v2)

**lmgame-Bench / GameArena (Hao AI Lab et al.; 21 May 2025)**
- Six games: Super Mario Bros, Tetris, Sokoban, Candy Crush, 2048 and Ace Attorney. The harness has perception (screen to text table or description), memory (last N states plus an o3-generated reflection) and optional long-CoT reasoning — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15146v2)
- **Without the harness, 40% of game runs failed to beat random; with it, 86.7% beat random.** Example: o4-mini Sokoban went from 1.3 to 5.3 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15146v2)
- With the harness, o3 scores Sokoban 8.0 / Mario 3445 / Tetris 42 / 2048 128 / Candy 647 / Ace Attorney 16. Gemini 2.5 Pro scores 4.3 / 1498 / 23.3 / 117.3 / 416.3 / 7.0; Claude 3.7 Sonnet (thinking) 2.3 / 1418.7 / 16.3 / 113.3 / 484.0 / 7.0. Random Mario is 987 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15146v2)

**Orak (KRAFTON et al.; 4 Jun 2025, v3 14 Apr 2026)**
- 12 games across 6 genres: Street Fighter III, Super Mario, Ace Attorney, Her Story, Pokémon Red, Darkest Dungeon, Minecraft, Stardew Valley, StarCraft II, Slay the Spire, Baba Is You and 2048. **Each game and each agentic module is an MCP server.** The default input is text state; action spaces range from 15-20 discrete fighting moves to joypad or high-level tools for Pokémon — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03610v3)
- Gemini 2.5 Pro has the best average rank (3.5, first in 5 of 12 games), GPT-5 and GPT-4o 3.6, Claude 3.7 5.1. **Image-only input caused "a substantial drop in performance"** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03610v3)

**LMAct (Google DeepMind; 2 Dec 2024, v3 23 May 2025)**
- Tests in-context imitation with up to 512 expert episodes (up to 1M tokens) on tic-tac-toe, chess, Atari, grid-world, crosswords and cheetah control. Models "generally fall short of expert performance", and more demonstrations often change little — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01441)

**UI-TARS-2 15-game browser suite (Sep 2025)**
- Mean normalised score (human = 100): **UI-TARS-2-RL 59.77, UI-TARS-2-SFT 44.27, OpenAI CUA 24.73, Claude Computer Use 21.61** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02544v2)
- On lmgame-Bench, UI-TARS-2 vs o3: Mario 1783 vs 1955, Tetris 16.0 vs 31.0, 2048 117.1 vs 128.2 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02544v2)

**Game-TARS (Oct 2025)**
- Minecraft MCU attack success rate (embodied / GUI / combat): Game-TARS-MoE-mini 72.0 / 55.4 / 66.1 vs UI-TARS-1.5 42.1 / 36.7 / 31.0. The paper claims it beats GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro and Claude-4-Sonnet on ViZDoom FPS maps, but the numbers are only in figures — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23691)

**PokeAgent Challenge (NeurIPS 2025 competition; paper Mar 2026)**
- The speedrun track is Pokémon Emerald up to the first gym, with 15 milestones. **The game runs in real time, so inference latency counts toward wall-clock time.** "Without a harness, raw frontier VLMs achieve effectively 0% task completion" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15563)
- Results: the winner, Heatz, took 40:13 using LLM-written scripted policies distilled into a neural network and refined with RL. The best pure-LLM harness took 1:29:17. Elite human: 18 min; average human 1:22:05. The best organiser baseline (Gemini 3 Flash) took about 2:24. Claude Sonnet 4.5 finished but varied from 6:25 to 20:45 at 3-4× the Gemini cost — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15563)
- **CLI-agent harnesses (Claude Code, Codex CLI, Gemini CLI) "lost coherence over thousands of decisions and misjudged their progress."** The baseline harness uses an orchestrator plus sub-agents, MCP tools for A* pathfinding, button inputs and knowledge retrieval, and auto-compaction — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15563)

**ARC-AGI-3 (ARC Prize; launched 25 Mar 2026)**
- An interactive, turn-based set of novel games with no instructions; "A 100% score means AI agents can beat every game as efficiently as humans" — [ARC Prize](https://arcprize.org/arc-agi/3/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621)
- At launch, frontier systems scored below 1% while humans solved 100% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621). Launch per-model figures (secondary): Gemini 3.1 Pro 0.37%, GPT-5.4 High 0.26%, Claude Opus 4.6 0.25% — [noqta.tn](https://noqta.tn/en/blog/arc-agi-3-agent-harness-benchmark-scores-2026)
- Later figures (secondary, not verified on arcprize.org): Claude Opus 5 at 30.2% verified; GPT-5.6 Sol 7.8% on the official harness vs 38.3% with OpenAI's reimplemented harness on the public set. OpenAI's harness retains reasoning across turns and uses compaction, where the official harness drops reasoning and truncates past roughly 175k characters. The noqta article also gives an unexplained 13.3% baseline — [noqta.tn](https://noqta.tn/en/blog/arc-agi-3-agent-harness-benchmark-scores-2026); OpenAI's post "How two settings tripled our ARC-AGI-3 scores" exists but returned 403 — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-two-settings-tripled-our-arc-agi-3-scores/)

**Other 2026 benchmarks**
- SpeedrunBench (Patronus AI, 2026; secondary via search) scores frames-to-goal across 10 retro and open-source games. On Pokémon Blue's first badge, the best agents are about 4× slower than the ~40,435-frame human record and the worst about 12× — [Patronus blog](https://patronus.ai/blog/introducing-speedrunbench-a-challenging-benchmark-for-frontier-agents); [preprint](https://cdn.patronus.ai/SpeedrunBench.pdf)
- CivBench (2 Sep 2026) plays Civilization VI through **76 MCP tools** plus a narration layer that converts visual state to text, over 300+ turns. Agents executed only 48.2-65.8% of their own stated commitments within 10 turns (RAG@10) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02459)
- The survey "AI for Games in the Foundation Model Era" (15 Sep 2026, 120 pages) finds evaluation most standardised for bounded game playing, and warns that control schemes and engine interfaces are setting-specific, so transfer claims need per-setting validation — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16679)

**Pokémon completions (community and vendor runs, not controlled benchmarks)**
- Gemini 2.5 Pro beat Pokémon Blue (May 2025): the first run took about 106,500 actions / ~816 h; a second run with Gemini 2.5 Pro 05-06 took 36,801 actions / 406 h — [GreaterWrong shortform](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/ekF2EDwKyZJNuxBTb/julian-bradshaw-s-shortform/comment/fB8atDtisJLWdHDSh). Gigazine gives 813 h for the first run — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/news/20250618-pokemon-gemini-panic)

## 3. Action interfaces: holds, mouse deltas, chunking, macros, emulator vs OS input, gamepads, frequency and real-time handling

### Takeaway
The trained agents converge on a low-level, timed action vocabulary. It has relative mouse deltas for camera control; explicit key state that persists across 33-100 ms chunks, so holds are expressed by repetition rather than a duration argument; and chunks of 6-16 actions per model call, at 20 Hz (VPT), 30 Hz (Lumine) or 10-20 Hz (Game-TARS). Harnesses for frontier models prefer coarser macros: navigate-to-tile, pathfinders, key_hold with a duration, emulator buttons. Pausing or freezing the game during inference is the dominant way to make real-time games tractable. NitroGen intercepts the engine clock, Cradle pauses RDR2, VideoGameBench Lite pauses the emulator, and Game-TARS sandboxes web games with time-pause. SIMA is the main real-time exception, and it compensates with latency-offset training and a Flash-Lite model.

### Cited Findings
- **Relative mouse deltas**: Lumine uses (ΔX, ΔY) in (-1000, 1000) executed over 200 ms, plus scroll ΔZ — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892). Game-TARS uses `mouseMove(dx, dy)` with |dx|,|dy| ≤ 180 — [Game-TARS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1). SIMA 2 uses "discretized relative (x, y) mouse movements" — [SIMA 2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04797v1). Cradle's mouse_move takes a speed, which "matters in games like RDR2" — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3)
- **Key state across chunks instead of duration arguments**: in Lumine, "unlisted keys are released, and keys in consecutive chunks stay held without re-pressing" — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892). In VideoGameBench's Game Boy interface, "repeating an action in the sequence maintains pressure on a button" — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- **Explicit duration holds** exist but confuse models. VideoGameBench's DOS interface offers `hold_key` with seconds (e.g. "A,1.5"), with defaults of 0.1 s for press_key and 0.5 s for hold_key, but the authors "found this to be confusing" for models because timing is hard to embed in context — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134). Anthropic's current computer-use toolset has `hold_key {text, duration}` and `wait {duration}`, each up to 300 s, plus `left_mouse_down` / `left_mouse_up` for drags that `left_click_drag` cannot express. Claude can batch several actions per turn — [Claude computer use tool docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool)
- **Separate down/up primitives**: Cradle exposes key_press, key_hold and key_release plus mouse hold/release — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3)
- **Chunk sizes and frequencies**: VPT 20 Hz — [VPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11795). Lumine 6 chunks × 33 ms per 200 ms step (30 Hz) — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892). SIMA 1 chunks of 8 actions — [SIMA 1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10179v2). NitroGen chunks of 16 gamepad actions — [NitroGen HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427). Game-TARS uses 50-100 ms action intervals — [Game-TARS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1)
- **Gamepad action space**: NitroGen uses 16 binary buttons plus 4 continuous stick axes — [NitroGen HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427). Muse/WHAM models controller actions for Bleeding Edge — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/introducing-muse). NitroGen is weaker on mouse/keyboard-heavy RTS and MOBA — [HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/aoiandroid/NitroGen)
- **Emulator button APIs**: VideoGameBench Game Boy actions are A, B, SELECT, START and the d-pad, with tuples for simultaneous presses. Each action lasts "roughly 1/4 second" in the standard prompt and "1/2 second or 30 frames" in Lite — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134). PokeAgent counts an action as each discrete button-press submission to an emulator running at a fixed frame rate — [PokeAgent HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15563)
- **Macro skills and code-as-action**: Voyager writes reusable code skills — [Voyager](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291). Cradle curates skills retrieved by embedding — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3). Claude's and Gemini's Pokémon navigators move multiple tiles per call — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7mqp8uRnnPdbBzJZE/is-gemini-now-better-than-claude-at-pokemon). PokeAgent's winner distilled LLM-written scripted policies — [PokeAgent HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15563)
- **Real-time handling**: Cradle pauses RDR2 and Stardew — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3). VideoGameBench Lite "pauses the emulator while the model thinks" — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134). NitroGen "intercepts the game engine's system clock" — [NitroGen HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427). Game-TARS uses "a sandbox with time-pause" — [Game-TARS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1). SIMA runs asynchronously with action-offset training — [SIMA 1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10179v2). In Real-Time Reasoning Gym, the environment advances every N generated tokens (32k/16k/8k/4k) and a default action is applied if none arrives in time — [RTR Gym HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04898v1)
- **Async dual-loop control** works under time pressure. At medium load, planning-only R1 drops from 0.92 (32k tokens per step) to 0.05 (4k), while AgileThinker drops only from 0.90 to 0.58. In wall-clock runs, AgileThinker scored 0.88 / 0.45 / 0.89 on Freeway / Snake / Overcooked against 0.12 / 0.04 / 0.00 for planning-only — [RTR Gym HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04898v1)
- **Thinking frequency**: Game-TARS "sparse thinking" scored 63% on Minecraft and 11.5 on Doom at 37 tokens per step. Always-on "greedy" thinking scored 45% and 7.2 at 68 tokens per step, and no thinking scored 55% and 13.2. Always-on reasoning adds latency and cascading errors in fast games like Doom — [Game-TARS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1)

### Inferences
- A server serving game-capable agents should let one call express a **timeline**: a list of (t_offset_ms, key_down/key_up/mouse_delta/button) events executed with ~10-33 ms precision. This mirrors Lumine and Game-TARS and is more expressive than a single duration argument. The existing `run_steps` (up to 30 steps, validated first) is the natural home; it would need per-step timing and raw key down/up events. (Server context: /Users/jameskaranja/Developer/projects/mcp-servers/computer-use-mcp/AGENTS.md describes `run_steps`, `hold_key` capped at 10 s, and `mouse_drag` interpolated with steps.)
- **Relative mouse motion** (raw deltas, not cursor warps) is the camera primitive every 3D agent uses. `mouse_drag` with interpolated steps partially covers this for button-held drags, but a free "move by (dx, dy) over T ms with N sub-steps, no button" primitive is what Lumine, SIMA and Game-TARS emit.
- Pausing is the single biggest lever for frontier models, but an OS-level server cannot intercept a game's clock the way NitroGen does. Practical substitutes are a configurable per-target "pause key" (e.g. Esc or P) pressed around reasoning, or OS process suspend/resume, which needs validation per game (see Gaps).
- A virtual gamepad output (16 buttons plus 2 sticks, matching NitroGen's layout) would open the gamepad-native genres where keyboard/mouse agents underperform.

### Gaps
- None of the sources reports whether OS-level synthetic input (macOS CGEventPost, Windows SendInput) is accepted by games that read raw input or DirectInput, or is blocked by anti-cheat. Lumine, SIMA and Game-TARS do not describe their input-injection layer in the text I read.
- I found no measurements of process-suspend (SIGSTOP / NtSuspendProcess) as a pause mechanism for commercial games. Cradle's pause method (in-game pause menu vs other) is not specified in the text I read.
- How NitroGen injects gamepad input into Windows games is not stated on the project page or in the HTML text.

## 4. Observation: capture frequency, video, OCR/minimap parsing, state APIs vs pixels, memory, grounding

### Takeaway
The trained agents use continuous low-resolution frames: 5 Hz at 720p (Lumine), 720p periodic latest frame (SIMA 2), and 256×256 single frame (NitroGen). Frontier harnesses gain from short multi-frame clips captured during and after the action (Cradle's 2 fps clip; VideoGameBench Lite's five frames 0.1 s apart for DOS) and from text state wherever it exists (RAM, minimaps, game APIs). Multiple benchmarks measure vision input hurting or not helping frontier models, so the best harnesses lean on structured text, overlays and persistent notes.

### Cited Findings
- Cradle feeds **a video of the last action at 2 fps** — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3). VideoGameBench gives a single frame 0.5 s after an action on the full benchmark. In Lite, Game Boy agents get the latest frame and **DOS agents get five past frames spaced 0.1 s apart**. Context is about 20 steps of frames and actions plus a textual scratchpad — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- Lumine uses a 20-frame sliding window at 5 Hz (ablation: 10 frames best, worse beyond). NitroGen found extra past frames gave no benefit over one — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892); [NitroGen HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427)
- **Vision often hurts frontier LLMs**: BALROG says "multimodal LLMs perform much worse when also presented with an image of the environment" (GPT-4o 32.34 → 22.56) — [BALROG HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13543v2). Orak says image-only input caused "a substantial drop in performance" — [Orak HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03610v3). Gemini 2.5 Pro played Pokémon "roughly as well as without the vision information", with RAM-extracted text doing the work — [dbreunig.com](https://www.dbreunig.com/2025/06/17/an-agentic-case-study-playing-pok%C3%A9mon-with-gemini.html)
- **Text perception modules**: lmgame-Bench converts grid games to text tables ("Box at (2,3)"), extracts Ace Attorney dialogue as text, and describes Mario frames in text — [lmgame-Bench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15146v2). CivBench's narration layer converts visual state to structured text — [CivBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02459). Gemini's harness gets a textual minimap and labels; Claude gets a grid overlay on screenshots — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7mqp8uRnnPdbBzJZE/is-gemini-now-better-than-claude-at-pokemon)
- **Frame synchronisation**: the Claude harness waits for the player to stop moving on spin tiles before sending the next screenshot — [harness excerpt](https://www.lesswrong.com/users/joshua-levine). VideoGameBench's human interface waits at least 0.5 s before updating the frame — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- **Memory**: Claude's Pokémon agent keeps notes outside context and re-reads them after resets — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents). Gemini's performance degraded "as the context grew significantly beyond 100k tokens", with a tendency to repeat actions from history — [dbreunig.com](https://www.dbreunig.com/2025/06/17/an-agentic-case-study-playing-pok%C3%A9mon-with-gemini.html). Lumine flushes context when it generates new reasoning, which serves as long-term memory — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892). SIMA 2 lists "a short memory, limited by the context window" as a limitation — [SIMA 2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04797v1)
- **Visual grounding failures in stylised games**: Lumine attributes "nearly half of all errors" to limited multimodal understanding (missing small objects, over-trusting vision over text) — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892). Cradle reports unreliable recognition of non-standard UI icons, mature crops in Stardew, and "out-of-distribution, non-realistic art styles" — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3). Claude Opus 4.5's "improved vision is still pretty bad" — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/gogZyeistdaDFuhbG/claude-plays-pokemon-opus-4-5-follow-up). VideoGameBench gives examples: Gemini 2.0 Flash shot an enemy it wrongly believed alive in Doom II, and GPT-4o believed it had already spoken to an NPC — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- **Resolution constraints of frontier computer use**: Claude Opus 4.7 and later accept up to 2,576 px on the long edge (4,784 visual tokens); earlier models 1,568 px and about 1.15 MP. The docs recommend a 1280×720 baseline when accuracy is poor — [Claude computer use tool docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool)

### Inferences
- A **multi-frame capture around an action** (e.g. N frames at a fixed interval during and after input, returned as a strip or a short clip) is the observation pattern most consistently tied to success for frontier harnesses: Cradle's clip, VideoGameBench's 5 × 0.1 s, Lumine's history window. The server's 62 ms warm capture (per AGENTS.md) makes 5-10 Hz bursts feasible on macOS.
- `wait_for_stable` (window stops changing) is the server-side equivalent of the Claude harness's "wait until the player stops moving". A region-scoped variant fits HUD-level waits.
- Because self-drawn games have no accessibility tree, OCR (`read_window_text`, `click_text`) plus optional grid overlays on screenshots (as in Claude's harness) are the general substitutes for the RAM and minimap text that made Gemini's Pokémon runs work.

### Gaps
- No source measured screenshot frequency against success for frontier models directly, for example 1 frame vs 5 frames per step on the same task.
- No published data on OCR accuracy for stylised game fonts and HUDs in these agent systems.

## 5. Latency, cost per action, and failure modes

### Takeaway
Trained small models act in ~0.1-0.25 s (Lumine). Frontier computer-use loops take seconds per step (UI-TARS-2 measured 2.5-4.0 s per round), which is why real-time play needs pausing or a fast policy. A VideoGameBench run costs up to ~$30, roughly $0.015 per step. The recurring failure modes are vision and grounding, a knowing-doing gap, navigation and spatial reasoning, mouse dragging, camera, combat and timing, context poisoning and loops, and hallucinated progress.

### Cited Findings
- **Lumine latency**: first action chunk 113.9 ms without reasoning, 234.0 ms with reasoning; average chunk decode 3.1 ms (max 12.4 ms); "a 25.3× overall latency reduction". Visual input is about 200 ms old when acted on — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892)
- **UI-TARS-2**: W4A8 quantisation raised generation from 29.6 to 47 tokens/s and cut latency "per interaction round from 4.0 to 2.5 seconds", while OSWorld dropped from 47.5 to 44.4 — [UI-TARS-2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02544v2)
- **Real-Time Reasoning Gym wall-clock fit**: T = 0.0473·N + 334.55 s for N tokens (R² = 0.9986), i.e. about 47 ms per generated token on their setup — [RTR Gym HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04898v1)
- **VideoGameBench cost per game run (USD)**: Claude 3.7 Sonnet $30.00 on Civilization I, $29.64 on Pokémon Crystal and $20.00 on Link's Awakening; Gemini 2.5 Pro $18.51 on Link's Awakening. Runs end early after about $30 (~2000 steps) without a new checkpoint, or 100+ steps on one screen — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- **PokeAgent**: Claude Sonnet 4.5 cost 3-4× the Gemini variants for the first-gym run; GPT-5.2 fell between them — [PokeAgent HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15563)
- Cradle does not report per-step latency or cost and notes "potentially high costs and long delays" — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3)
- **Knowing-doing gap**: VideoGameBench models see the Zelda exit at the bottom of the screen but keep pressing "down" regardless of position — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134). BALROG models exit the dungeon or eat rotten food despite stating that both are dangerous — [BALROG HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13543v2)
- **Mouse precision**: on VideoGameBench mouse dragging, every model scored 0-10% — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- **Navigation and spatial reasoning**: Cradle's RDR2 indoor search showed "aimless circling" from poor spatial perception, and rugged terrain hurt path-following — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3). Lumine shows hesitation, poor stamina management and mistimed actions — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892). Pathfinding is "very hard for both LLMs (mostly due to vision problems)" — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7mqp8uRnnPdbBzJZE/is-gemini-now-better-than-claude-at-pokemon). PokeAgent names "VLM-SLAM" spatial localisation as an open problem — [PokeAgent HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15563)
- **Combat and timing**: Cradle shows weak real-time combat, enemy localisation and action timing in RDR2 — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3). Lumine has "a zero success rate against the Eye of the Storm" — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892). SIMA 2 says combat is especially weak, partly due to motor difficulty, and lists precise low-level keyboard/mouse control as a limitation — [SIMA 2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04797v1)
- **Action-space and prior failures (Game-TARS)**: the model outputs pre-training keys (Space for jump) when the prompt says otherwise; it shows behavioural inertia and repetitive-action bias; and it gets into hallucinated reasoning loops that stop early — [Game-TARS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1)
- **Context poisoning, panic and fixation (Gemini 2.5)**: it hunted for a TEA item that exists only in the remake, held a hallucinated goal in its scratchpad, deliberately blacked out to teleport, and in "panic" sometimes "completely forgot to use it's [sic] pathfinder tool" — [dbreunig.com](https://www.dbreunig.com/2025/06/17/an-agentic-case-study-playing-pok%C3%A9mon-with-gemini.html)
- **Memory and planning**: in VideoGameBench, GPT-4o overwrote its scratchpad in Doom II and forgot its delivery objective in Pokémon Crystal — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134). Claude Opus 4.5 skipped the Gold Teeth, which later blocked Victory Road — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/gogZyeistdaDFuhbG/claude-plays-pokemon-opus-4-5-follow-up). CivBench agents executed only 48.2-65.8% of their own 10-turn commitments — [CivBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02459)

### Inferences
- At $30 per ~2,000 steps (VideoGameBench), frontier pixel play costs on the order of $0.015 per step, and a full RPG run at tens of thousands of steps costs hundreds of dollars. This is a reason to batch motor actions per call (chunks, macros) instead of one action per screenshot.
- Several failure modes are partly server-addressable. Overlays and OCR help grounding; frame-synced capture helps "acted on a stale frame"; a relative-move primitive helps camera control; an effect report after each action (the server's `effect` field) helps the knowing-doing gap. Others (planning, memory, context poisoning) belong to the harness.

### Gaps
- No public per-action latency for Claude or OpenAI computer-use models specifically in game settings was found.
- SIMA 2 and Game-TARS do not report end-to-end action latency in the text I read.

## 6. Lessons for a general desktop computer-use MCP server: which primitives the best agents depend on

### Takeaway
The best agents depend on six things: (1) timed, chunked low-level input with explicit key-down/up state and holds; (2) relative mouse deltas for cameras; (3) the ability to stop the world (pause or freeze) while the model thinks; (4) frame-synced, multi-frame capture after an action rather than a single delayed screenshot; (5) text state or overlays in place of raw pixels when available; and (6) navigation and memory helpers above the input layer. The first four are server primitives. The fifth is partly server (OCR, grid overlays) and partly per-game integration (MCP game servers, as in Orak and CivBench). The sixth is harness. Gamepad output is the main gap for console-style games.

### Cited Findings
- Timed, chunked input is the action format of every high-performing trained game agent: Lumine at 30 Hz with 33 ms chunks and persistent key state — [Lumine HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08892); Game-TARS at 50-100 ms — [Game-TARS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23691v1); VPT at 20 Hz — [VPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11795); SIMA in 8-action chunks — [SIMA 1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10179v2)
- Hold-and-release semantics appear in general computer-use APIs (`hold_key` with duration, `left_mouse_down` / `left_mouse_up`) — [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool) — and in Cradle (key_hold / key_release) — [Cradle HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186v3). Free-text hold durations confused models in VideoGameBench — [VideoGameBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18134)
- Pausing is used by Cradle, VideoGameBench Lite, Game-TARS and NitroGen (citations in section 3). PokeAgent's real-time track shows what happens without it: raw frontier VLMs reach ~0% — [PokeAgent HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15563)
- Structured state beats pixels for frontier models: lmgame-Bench, BALROG, Orak and the Gemini RAM extraction (citations in sections 2 and 4). Game-specific MCP servers are the integration pattern in Orak ("each game environment and agentic module runs as an independent MCP server") — [Orak HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03610v3) — and in CivBench (76 MCP tools) — [CivBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02459)
- Harness-level helpers (A* pathfinding, sub-agents, knowledge retrieval, auto-compaction) are required for long-horizon play, and generic CLI-agent harnesses "lost coherence over thousands of decisions" — [PokeAgent HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15563)
- Retaining reasoning across turns and compacting rather than truncating history reportedly raised one model's ARC-AGI-3 score roughly 5× (secondary) — [noqta.tn](https://noqta.tn/en/blog/arc-agi-3-agent-harness-benchmark-scores-2026)
- Gamepad-native genres are where keyboard/mouse lags: NitroGen targets them, and Muse models controller actions — [NitroGen HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02427); [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/introducing-muse)

### Inferences
Priority list for the server, ordered by evidence strength. These map to the existing surface described in /Users/jameskaranja/Developer/projects/mcp-servers/computer-use-mcp/AGENTS.md (7.7.0):
1. **Input timeline primitive.** Extend `run_steps`, or add a `input_sequence`, with raw `key_down` / `key_up` / `mouse_move_relative {dx, dy, duration_ms, steps}` / `button_down` / `button_up` events at millisecond offsets, executed by the native layer with ~10-33 ms jitter. This matches Lumine, Game-TARS and VPT, and lets a frontier model emit one 0.5-3 s "chunk" per call instead of one key per screenshot.
2. **Relative mouse movement without a button.** Today `mouse_drag` interpolates paths with a button held. Camera control in 3D games needs button-free deltas, and on games that capture the cursor these likely must be raw relative events, not absolute warps (needs per-platform verification).
3. **Capture bound to the action.** Return K frames at a fixed interval during and after the timeline (e.g. 5 frames × 100 ms, as in VideoGameBench Lite DOS), or a 2 fps clip as in Cradle, in the same call. Keep `wait_for_stable` as the "until motion stops" sync, as in Claude's Pokémon harness.
4. **Pause/resume hooks.** A per-target configurable pause binding pressed before the model reasons and released after, and optionally OS-level process suspend for single-player titles. Measure per game, because NitroGen-style clock interception is not available to an OS-level server.
5. **Virtual gamepad output.** Expose a NitroGen-shaped action (16 buttons plus 2 analogue sticks plus triggers) through an OS virtual controller where available.
6. **Text perception aids.** Grid or coordinate overlays on screenshots (the Claude navigator's model), region OCR for HUDs and dialogue (already present on macOS), and a documented path to delegate to game-specific MCP servers (Orak and CivBench pattern) when a game exposes state.
7. **Leave planning, pathfinding and memory to the harness, but keep them cheap.** The `effect` / `next_call` reporting, and the "unchanged capture" short-circuit, reduce token spend on long loops where cost is about $0.015 per step.

### Gaps
- No source compares OS-level synthetic input against in-engine or emulator input for the same agent and game, so the reliability cost of a general desktop server versus game-specific hooks is unquantified.
- No source tests whether frontier models can use a millisecond-timeline action format well. Lumine and Game-TARS are trained on it; VideoGameBench found explicit durations confusing. Whether models can author timelines zero-shot, or need macros (e.g. "walk forward 1.2 s while turning 30°"), is open.
- Anti-cheat and kernel-level input filtering for commercial online games were not covered by any source found.
